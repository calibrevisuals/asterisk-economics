$6 Billion Was Stolen. Customers Lost Nothing. How?

The Binance theft of 29 October 2026 (about $6.3 billion) followed from the customer's seat: what stands behind coins on an exchange, Binance's safety fund, the stablecoin freezes, the company's own money, and what is different from a bank or a brokerage in the US

Every fact in this episode comes from the sources below.

Primary sources

  • Elliptic, "Binance: the largest hack in history" (updated 1 Nov 2026)The timeline (20:05 UTC on 29 Oct, the $2.53 billion transaction at 20:24 UTC), Binance's statement that it reserved more than $6.34 billion of company funds, the Tether and Circle freezes (37 minutes; $2.85 billion, 44%, frozen or seized), and the FBI's attribution.

  • FDIC, "Understanding Deposit Insurance"$250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category, if a bank fails; crypto is not covered.

  • SIPC, "What SIPC Protects"Up to $500,000 per customer when a brokerage firm fails; crypto that is not a registered security is not covered.

References

  • Al Jazeera, "Binance says $6.3bn stolen in biggest crypto heist in history: what we know" (30 Oct 2026)aljazeera.com

  • Reuters via Honolulu Star-Advertiser, "Binance clients pull $4.7 billion after hack" (2 Nov 2026)staradvertiser.com

  • Bloomberg Law, "Binance lacks cyber insurance coverage for $6.3 billion hack" (3 Nov 2026)news.bloomberglaw.com

  • 24/7 Wall St, "Binance has replaced 98% of the hacked coins" (5 Nov 2026)247wallst.com

What this video does not claim: "customers lost nothing" is Binance's own promise and account, backed by the replacement figures it reported; it is not a legal guarantee. The FBI says North Korea was behind the theft; North Korea denies it. The split between Binance's safety fund and its own money is our estimate, because Binance has not published it. Whether the frozen stablecoins come back to Binance is not confirmed. Nothing here says any exchange is safe or unsafe, and nothing here is advice.
