Where Did All the Small-Town Banks Go?

How America went from about 18,000 banks in 1986 to 4,238 in 2026: the old one-office rules that made a bank in every town, the laws that lifted them, where the banks went (bought, merged into sister banks, failed), why so few new banks opened, what happens to a branch after a merger, what a town loses when its last branch closes, and where branches open now

Every fact in this episode comes from the sources below.

Primary sources

  • FDIC, Quarterly Banking Profile, second quarter 2026 (press release)4,238 FDIC-insured banks and savings institutions in Q2 2026, the count used in the video.

  • Congressional Research Service, IN11062 (2019)18,083 insured banks and savings institutions in 1986, the peak.

  • FDIC, Quarterly Banking Profile historical trends, fourth quarter 2025Year-by-year bank counts and failures, including the end-of-2007 count and the 465 failures from 2008 to 2012.

  • FDIC, Community Banking Study 2012, chapter 2: Structural ChangeWhere the 15,432 banks that left the industry from 1984 to 2011 went: 49% merged with an unaffiliated bank, 32% folded into a bank in the same holding company, 17% failed. Branching rules by state (16 states in 1984, 40 by 1994), the 1994 interstate law, merger waves, and the four largest banks' share of branches.

  • Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago, Chicago Fed Letter 238 (May 2007)Illinois' branching ban from the 1870 constitution, the 1967 rule allowing one drive-up within 1,500 feet, and the later steps to full branching.

  • Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond, Econ Focus Q1 2016, "Who Wants to Start a Bank?"About 159 new banks a year from 1997 to 2007; Bank of Bird-in-Hand (2013) as the first new bank in three years; low rates and the growth of rules and reporting (the Call Report) as reasons.

  • Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond, Econ Focus Q4 2013, "Why Was Canada Exempt from the Financial Crisis?"Canada's nationwide branch banking and its record through the Great Depression.

  • Bordo, Redish and Rockoff, "Why didn't Canada have a banking crisis in 2008 (or in 1930, or 1907, or ...)?", NBER Working Paper 17312Canada's branch-banking system and its banks through the Depression.

  • FDIC, Community Banking Study 2020, chapter 4Community banks held about 15% of bank loans but 36% of small-business loans and 70% of farm loans (2019).

  • FDIC, "Your bank branch is relocating or closing" (July 2024)A bank must mail customers a notice at least 90 days before it closes a branch.

  • FDIC, Deposit insurance when insured banks mergeDeposits from the bought bank stay separately insured for six months after a merger; the $250,000 limit.

  • NCRC, "Bank branch trends for 2026, 3rd quarter: the growth streak ends" (26 Oct 2026)446 branches closed and 313 opened in Q3 2026, ending three quarters of net gains; JPMorgan Chase opened 266 and closed 99 over five quarters; growth in Southeast and Southwest metros.

What this video does not claim: Drain's branch was U.S. Bank's own closure, not the result of a merger. Rural branches did not close faster than city ones; the difference is how far the next branch is. Experts disagree on how much of the drop in new banks came from low interest rates and how much from rules; the video gives both. The 2026 run of branch openings ended in the third quarter. No party, politician or company is blamed, and nothing here is advice on where to keep your money.
