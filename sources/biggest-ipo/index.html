What Happened to Everyone Who Bought the Biggest IPO Ever

SpaceX's record IPO in June 2026 and the five biggest IPOs before it (Visa, GM, Facebook, Alibaba, Saudi Aramco): who got the IPO price, who bought at the open, who held on, and what Anthropic's filing means for the next one

Every fact in this episode comes from the sources below.

Primary sources

  • Anthropic, PBC, Form S-1 registration statement (SEC EDGAR, filed 26 Oct 2026)Revenue of $386 million in 2024 and $16.3 billion in the first half of 2026, the first-half loss of about $81 billion with the $72 billion accounting charge, and the staged insider lockups.

  • Jay R. Ritter, University of Florida, "Initial Public Offerings: Updated Statistics" (5 Oct 2026)The average first-day jump of US IPOs from 1980 to 2025.

  • Jay R. Ritter, University of Florida, "Long-run returns on IPOs"Three-year returns after the first-day close, against the whole market and similar-sized companies.

What this video does not claim: it does not say every big IPO loses money (Visa rose and Aramco stayed about flat), and it does not say insiders sold in bad faith. SpaceX's slide came before its first lockup ended, not because of it; the stock rose that day. Anthropic's raise of up to $100 billion is a news report, not a fact from the filing, and nothing here is a forecast or advice to buy or sell. The six IPOs are the biggest and most hyped of all, so they illustrate the pattern; they don't prove a rule.
