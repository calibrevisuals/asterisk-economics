WTF Happened to the American Economy in 1950s?

Why America's economy boomed from 1945 to 1973: rivals in ruins after the war, the Treaty of Detroit and the 1950s paycheck, selling to a rebuilding world, then Germany and Japan catching up and pay splitting from output

Every fact in this episode comes from the sources below.

Primary sources

  • Maddison Project Database 2023, University of GroningenEach country's share of world output: the US about 27% in 1950 (our sum of GDP per person times population), Germany about 5%, Japan 3%, the US about 15% today.

  • Economic Policy Institute, The Productivity-Pay GapOutput per hour up about 97% and typical pay up about 91% from 1948 to 1973; about 65% against 8% from 1979 to 2013. Some economists debate the method.

  • US Census Bureau, Historical Income Tables: FamiliesMedian family income in today's dollars: about $36,000 in 1947 and about $73,000 in 1973.

  • Congressional Research Service, report 98-536, US trade balance historyA US goods trade surplus every year from 1948 to 1970 ($5.5 billion in 1948), and the first postwar deficit in 1971.

  • US Bureau of Labor Statistics, Beyond the Numbers: Forty years of falling manufacturing employmentFactories held about a third of nonfarm jobs in the 1950s; factory jobs peaked at about 19.6 million in 1979 and were about 12.8 million in 2019.

  • US State Department, Office of the Historian: Marshall Plan, 1948More than $12 billion to rebuild Western Europe, and the plan's help to the US economy by building markets for American goods.

  • Federal Highway Administration, history of the Eisenhower Interstate SystemThe 1956 law for 41,000 miles of interstate highways, the biggest public works project in American history at the time.

What this video does not claim: it does not say the 1950s were better for everyone (Black families were shut out of much of the housing boom), and it does not say high taxes or unions alone caused the boom. America's 27% is our own sum from the Maddison data, not half the world's economy. The house-price comparison uses two different data series, so it is rounded. The American economy kept growing after 1973; what fell was its share of the world and how much of the growth reached pay.
